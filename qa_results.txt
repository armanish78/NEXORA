Solar energy storage is crucial for managing the time-dependent and intermittent nature of solar energy. According to [BME654B-module-2-pdf.pdf, Page 31], solar energy is a time-dependent and intermittent resource, while energy demands are also time-dependent. This necessitates the storage of energy so that solar energy can meet energy needs, especially during periods of low solar energy availability, such as winter heating requirements. 

Storage systems can be designed to capture solar energy when insolation is high and release it when demand is greatest, as mentioned in [BME654B-module-2-pdf.pdf, Page 31]. Additionally, storage allows for delivering power during times when insolation is insufficient, [BME654B-module-2-pdf.pdf, Page 31]. Furthermore, storage enables the delivery of short peaks of power exceeding the rated capacity of the solar system, [BME654B-module-2-pdf.pdf, Page 31].

Optimally designed solar systems can improve the reliability of both solar thermal and solar electric systems, [BME654B-module-2-pdf.pdf, Page 31]. They also facilitate a better match between solar energy input and load demand output, [BME654B-module-2-pdf.pdf, Page 31]. The optimal capacity of an energy storage system depends on various factors including the expected time dependence of solar radiation availability, nature of loads, required reliability, method of auxiliary energy supply, size of the solar thermal power system or solar-electric generator, cost per kWhr of stored energy, permissible capital cost allocated to storage, and environmental and safety considerations, [BME654B-module-2-pdf.pdf, Page 31].

There are two primary methods of solar energy storage: thermal and chemical. Thermal storage involves heating, melting, or vaporizing materials to store energy, which can be released as heat when needed. Sensible heat storage occurs when a material's temperature rises, whereas latent heat storage happens during phase changes like melting or boiling, [BME654B-module-2-pdf.pdf, Page 32]. Commonly used for domestic water and space heating applications and thermal power applications, [BME654B-module-2-pdf.pdf, Page 32].

Water storage is one method of thermal energy storage, where water is the most common heat transfer fluid in solar systems. Water is stored directly in an insulated tank, with energy added by circulating water through collectors and removed by circulating water through the load. The optimal tank size for flat-plate collector systems is 70 kg/m2 (2 gal/ft2), [BME654B-module-2-pdf.pdf, Page 33]. Water is an inexpensive, readily available, and useful material for storing sensible heat, [BME654B-module-2-pdf.pdf, Page 33].

Another method of storage is chemical storage, specifically in the form of fuel. Here, the reactant in a storage battery is generated by a photochemical reaction due to solar radiation. The battery is charged photochemically and discharged electrically as needed, [BME654B-module-2-pdf.pdf, Page 38].
